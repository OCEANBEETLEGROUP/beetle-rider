Kamakura looks small on a map and rides big in real life — temples in the hills, beaches on the bay, and Enoshima glittering offshore. Walking it means choosing. Buses mean queueing. A **bike rental in Kamakura** solves it, and an e-bike solves the part nobody warns you about: the hills.

## Why E-Bike Beats Everything Else Here

Kamakura's sights are spread in a triangle: the station area, the Great Buddha to the west, the coast to the south. The connecting roads climb short, sharp hills that turn regular rental bicycles into pushing exercises. Our e-bikes flatten them — max 20 km/h, zero effort, full grin.

## The Route (4-Hour Cruise Plan, ¥5,000)

**Start — BEETLE RIDER, 4 min from the station.** Quick setup, saddle check, and you're rolling. ([Where to find us](/en/access/))

**1. Tsurugaoka Hachimangu (5 min).** Kamakura's grand shrine. Park at the torii, walk the approach, watch the city line up behind you from the top of the steps.

**2. The Great Buddha of Kotoku-in (15 min).** Cut west through the lanes to the 13-meter bronze Buddha who has been sitting through typhoons since 1252. This is the leg where the e-bike earns its keep — the hill by the tunnel is no joke on a regular bicycle.

**3. Hase & Yuigahama Beach (5 min).** Roll downhill to the sand. In summer the beach houses are open; off-season it's you, the surfers, and the hawks.

**4. The Coastal Road to Enoshima (20 min).** The best twenty minutes of the day. Ocean on your left the entire way along Route 134's cycling side, with Enoshima growing bigger ahead of you. Lunch on the island — shirasu bowls are the local rite.

**5. Return along the coast (25 min).** Same road, reversed light. If you timed it right, the sun is dropping toward Fuji as you ride home.

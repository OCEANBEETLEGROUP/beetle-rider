You're coming to Japan and you want a day on a motorcycle. Not a tour bus, not a group ride behind a guide — a bike, a road, and your own pace.

Here's what that takes, and what you'd actually be riding.

## What You Need to Ride Here

Japan recognises an International Driving Permit issued under the **1949 Geneva Convention**, and for a motorcycle it has to carry **Category A**. Three documents, every time:

1. **IDP (1949 Geneva format)** — issued in your home country *before* you fly. This is the one people miss.
2. **Your home licence** — the physical original
3. **Your passport**

Some countries never issued the 1949 version — Germany, France, Switzerland, Belgium, Monaco, Estonia and Taiwan among them. You're not shut out: an official Japanese translation of your licence works instead, and we've had plenty of German and Swiss riders come through on exactly that. The [full breakdown is here](/en/journal/japan-license-idp-guide/).

Sort this out at home. It's the only part of the trip you can't fix once you've landed.

## What You'd Be Riding

Every machine in the garage is a one-off custom chopper. Not a rental-fleet commuter with a sticker on the tank.

- **Evolution Sportster 883, '88–'99.** Among the more manageable Harleys, and easy enough to live with day to day.
- **Hardtail rigids.** Rear suspension removed, the whole bike resolving into a single line. You feel the road. That's the point.
- **Named builders, named painters.** Each bike credits the people who made it. If you want the background on why Japan builds these the way it does, we wrote [a piece on it](/en/journal/japanese-chopper-culture/).

You will not see another one of these on the road. That's not marketing — they're one-offs.

## Where It Is

Kamakura, on the coast south of Tokyo. **One hour from Tokyo Station on the JR Yokosuka line — one train, no transfer, around ¥950.** From Shinjuku, the Shonan-Shinjuku line, about the same. The garage is a four-minute walk from Kamakura Station.

It's an easy morning out of a Tokyo hotel, and most of our riders treat it exactly that way.

## The Roads

Turn right out of the garage and you're on **Route 134**: ocean on one side the whole way, Enoshima ahead, Mt. Fuji behind it on a clear day. No warm-up, no getting out of a city first.

If you take the bike for more than a day, the other direction is worth it too — **the Shuto Expressway after dark**, the Bay Bridge, the C1 loop through the building canyons, Daikoku PA after midnight. Tokyo is genuinely better from a motorcycle at 11pm than it is on foot at any hour. [Route guide here](/en/journal/tokyo-night-run-guide/).

## Booking, in English

The site, the booking, the paperwork, the briefing — all of it in English. No Japanese needed at any point, and no Japanese phone number or address required.

- **Day rides from ¥22,000.** Multi-day drops sharply per day; the long bookings are where the value is.
- **Free cancellation until five days before**, so booking early costs you nothing.
- **Book before you fly** — partly for availability on weekends, mostly because the IDP has to be issued at home anyway.

## A Day, Start to Finish

1. Book online from home, IDP in hand.
2. Train from Tokyo or Shinjuku. About an hour, one seat.
3. Four-minute walk. Paperwork and briefing, twenty minutes.
4. Coast road, as far as you feel like going. Back whenever the day's done.
